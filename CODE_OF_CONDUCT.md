# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

This project exists to make splicing analysis easier to do and easier to check.
Everyone taking part — asking a question, reporting a bug, reviewing a change —
is expected to keep the discussion about the work.

Expected of participants:

- Assume competence and good faith. People come to this project from wet-lab
  biology, bioinformatics and software engineering; a question that is obvious in
  one of those backgrounds is often not obvious in another.
- Be specific. "This is wrong" helps nobody; "this classifies a novel donor as
  annotated when the GTF has overlapping transcripts" can be acted on.
- Accept that scientific disagreement is normal and welcome. Disagree with the
  method, the assumption or the code — not with the person.
- Respect the effort behind a contribution, including the effort behind a
  contribution you are rejecting.

Not acceptable:

- Personal attacks, insults or demeaning remarks.
- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing others' private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions or reviews.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages and code review — and when representing the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behaviour to **elmasnrylmz@gmail.com**. Reports are read by
the maintainer and handled confidentially. You will get a response, and you will
not be asked to justify why the behaviour bothered you.

Depending on severity, the response may be a private warning, a request for a
public apology, removal of the offending content, or a temporary or permanent
ban from the project's spaces. The maintainer will not act on a report by
retaliating against the reporter.

## Attribution

The standards above follow the spirit of the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org), adapted for a
small scientific software project.
